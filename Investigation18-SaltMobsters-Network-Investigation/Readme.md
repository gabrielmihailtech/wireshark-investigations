# Investigation 18 - SaltMobsters Network Investigation

## Objective

Investigate suspicious network activity within the SALTMOBSTERS environment, identify the compromised workstation, and document observed indicators of compromise (IOCs) and suspicious communications.

---

## Executive Summary

Network traffic analysis identified a Windows workstation within the SALTMOBSTERS environment that generated significant communications with external infrastructure.

The primary workstation under investigation was identified as DESKTOP-NIEE9LP. Analysis revealed persistent communications with external IP address `65.60.35.141`, which represented the largest external conversation observed within the capture.

Additional communications were observed with `185.66.15.228` and `23.47.52.173`. TLS analysis of the latter identified Akamai CDN infrastructure, suggesting that communication was likely legitimate and unrelated to the primary suspicious activity.

The investigation focused on the workstation's communications with external infrastructure and unusual NetBIOS name resolution behavior.

## Findings

- Host 10.6.15.119 was identified as the primary workstation involved in the activity.
- NBNS traffic identified the workstation as DESKTOP-NIEE9LP.
- DNS traffic confirmed membership in the SALTMOBSTERS domain.
- The host generated significant communications with external IP address 65.60.35.141.
- Additional communications occurred with 185.66.15.228 and 23.47.52.173.
- TLS inspection identified Akamai infrastructure associated with 23.47.52.173.
- The workstation generated numerous unusual NetBIOS name queries that appeared automated in nature.
- The observed behavior is suspicious and may indicate malware-related network activity or automated host discovery behavior.

---

## Conclusion

The investigation identified workstation DESKTOP-NIEE9LP (10.6.15.119) as the primary host involved in the suspicious activity within the SALTMOBSTERS environment.

The system maintained substantial communications with external infrastructure, particularly 65.60.35.141, while simultaneously generating a large number of unusual NetBIOS name queries. 
Although TLS analysis identified legitimate Akamai infrastructure for one destination, the overall activity remains suspicious due to the volume of external communications and abnormal NetBIOS behavior.

The identified external IP addresses and unusual NetBIOS query patterns should be considered indicators of interest for further monitoring and threat hunting activities.
